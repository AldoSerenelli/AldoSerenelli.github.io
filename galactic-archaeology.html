Galactic Archaeology

Connecting the properties of individual stars with the formation and evolution of stellar populations.

Stellar ages as a bridge

Stellar ages provide a chronology for understanding how the Milky Way formed and evolved. My main contribution to Galactic archaeology is to help collaborators obtain more reliable ages, combining stellar evolution models with spectroscopic observations and, where available, asteroseismology.

This work includes applications of BeSPP to Galactic stellar populations and contributions to the development of SAPP — the Stellar Abundances and atmospheric Parameters Pipeline. SAPP combines different types of observations to determine stellar parameters and chemical abundances, connecting the characterization of individual stars with studies of Galactic history.

Surveys and collaborations

I have been a long-standing member of the APOKASC collaboration, combining APOGEE spectroscopy with Kepler asteroseismology to determine stellar properties and ages.

I am also a member of 4MIDABLE-HR, the 4MOST S4 high-resolution survey of the Galactic disc and bulge, where I lead the asteroseismic subsurveys. My involvement connects stellar age determination with the survey’s studies of Galactic structure and evolution through stellar chemistry and motions.

I have also contributed to SAPP’s development for use within the 4MOST analysis pipeline. A version of SAPP contributes to PLATO’s spectroscopic analysis module, determining effective temperatures, metallicities and other atmospheric parameters.

Selected Work

The Prince and the Pauper: Evidence for the early high-redshift formation of the Galactic α-poor disc population — Gent et al. (2024)

Combines Gaia-ESO spectroscopy with Gaia observations to investigate the chemistry, ages and motions of Milky Way disc stars. The identification of α-poor stars with ages reaching approximately 11 billion years suggests that the thin disc formed early and evolved alongside the thick disc.

The SAPP pipeline for the determination of stellar abundances and atmospheric parameters of stars in the core program of the PLATO mission — Gent et al. (2022)

Introduces a Bayesian framework combining spectroscopy, photometry, astrometry and asteroseismology to determine stellar properties. Tests on benchmark stars demonstrate how combining these observations improves parameter estimates and resolves degeneracies.

DOIarXiv

Two chemically similar stellar overdensities on opposite sides of the plane of the Galactic disk — Bergemann et al. (2018)

Chemical abundances of stars in Triangulum–Andromeda and A13 closely resemble those of the Milky Way disc, supporting a common Galactic origin. These findings suggest that the two structures formed through disturbances of the disc by passing or merging satellite galaxies.

DOIarXiv

The Gaia-ESO Survey: radial metallicity gradients and age-metallicity relation of stars in the Milky Way disk — Bergemann et al. (2014)

Stellar ages and chemical abundances reveal relationships between age, metallicity and Galactic location, accounting for selection effects in the Gaia-ESO Survey. The results show substantial metallicity scatter at any age and identify differences between the inner and outer disc that constrain models of Galactic evolution.
