Stellar Physics
& Asteroseismology

Using oscillations and spectroscopy to test stellar models and determine the ages and fundamental parameters of stars.

From observations to stellar interiors

My work on low- and intermediate-mass stars combines stellar evolution calculations with asteroseismic and spectroscopic observations. These constraints allow models to be tested and help improve estimates of stellar masses, radii and ages.

Space observations from Kepler and TESS, and the oncoming PLATO mission, provide the context for much of this work. The challenge is to translate precise measurements into reliable physical inferences, while understanding the assumptions and degeneracies in stellar models.

Testing the models

Selected studies address the degeneracy between stellar mass and initial helium abundance, the reliability of models at low metallicity, and prescriptions for stellar convection. Other work connects asteroseismic modeling with the characterization of stars hosting planets.

BeSPP

I developed the Bellaterra Stellar Parameters Pipeline (BeSPP), which I have used extensively in my research on solar-like stars. Two separate codes share this name: one uses global asteroseismic parameters, while the other analyzes individual oscillation frequencies.

BeSPP originated in my work on inferring stellar properties from spectroscopic inputs, described in Serenelli et al. (2013). It was subsequently extended to include asteroseismic quantities, with the name BeSPP first appearing in Chaplin et al. (2014).

Connections with spectroscopic surveys

Through my leadership of the asteroseismic subsurveys within 4MOST S4, I also connect stellar modelling and asteroseismology with large spectroscopic surveys. Further details are given under Galactic Archaeology & Stellar Populations.

Selected Work

APOKASC-3: The Third Joint Spectroscopic and Asteroseismic Catalog for Evolved Stars in the Kepler Fields — Pinsonneault et al. (2025)

Stellar masses, radii and ages for evolved Kepler stars are determined by combining asteroseismology, APOGEE spectroscopy and Gaia-based calibration. The catalogue enables precise studies of Galactic stellar populations, including the age distribution of the chemical thick disc and mass loss during red-giant evolution.

Stellar Models are Reliable at Low Metallicity: An Asteroseismic Age for the Ancient Very Metal-Poor Star KIC 8144907 — Huber et al. (2024)

Detailed modeling of individual oscillation frequencies establishes an age of approximately 12 billion years for KIC 8144907, testing stellar models at very low metallicity. Agreement among independent modeling approaches supports the reliability of these age estimates and provides an observational anchor for the early history of the Milky Way.

DOIarXiv

Age dating of an early Milky Way merger via asteroseismology of the naked-eye star ν Indi — Chaplin et al. (2020)

The age of the halo star ν Indi, determined using TESS asteroseismology and complementary observations, constrains the timing of the Gaia–Enceladus merger. Its age and motion indicate that it belonged to the Milky Way before the collision, providing a stellar clock for this episode of Galactic assembly.

DOIarXiv

Two chemically similar stellar overdensities on opposite sides of the plane of the Galactic disk — Bergemann et al. (2018)

Chemical abundances of stars in Triangulum–Andromeda and A13 closely resemble those of the Milky Way disc, supporting a common Galactic origin. These findings suggest that the two structures formed through disturbances of the disc by passing or merging satellite galaxies.

DOIarXiv

The First APOKASC Catalog of Kepler Dwarf and Subgiant Stars — Serenelli et al. (2017)

Stellar properties for 415 dwarfs and subgiants are determined by combining Kepler asteroseismology with APOGEE spectroscopy and assessing statistical and systematic uncertainties. Comparisons with independent measurements demonstrate the reliability of global-seismology estimates, while highlighting the importance of accurate temperatures and metallicities.

DOIarXiv

The Gaia-ESO Survey: radial metallicity gradients and age-metallicity relation of stars in the Milky Way disk — Bergemann et al. (2014)

Stellar ages and chemical abundances reveal relationships between age, metallicity and Galactic location, accounting for selection effects in the Gaia-ESO Survey. The results show substantial metallicity scatter at any age and identify differences between the inner and outer disc that constrain models of Galactic evolution.
